From SLIIT Classrooms to the World's Top 2% The Journey of Prof. Bhagya Nathali Silva

An alumna who returned as an acclaimed academic, Professor Bhagya Nathali Silva exemplifies how dedication and research can create global impact.

There's something quietly remarkable about Professor Bhagya Nathali Silva's story. She began her academic journey at SLIIT, completing her undergraduate degree in Information Technology in 2011, and her master’s at the very same institution by 2015. Years later, she would return to those same halls — not as a student, but as a globally recognized researcher in her field.

After her postgraduate studies, Prof. Silva pursued her PhD in Computer Science and Engineering at Kyungpook National University in South Korea, under the supervision of Prof. Kijun Han. Several thousands of miles away from home and 2 scholarships later she continued as a postdoctoral fellow at Kyungpook National University after her PhD.

It was the kind of path that required both courage and conviction, and it paid off. Prof. Silva returned to Sri Lanka, bringing with her a wealth of international research experience and a clear sense of purpose.

Today, she is a Professor in the Faculty of Computing at SLIIT, where her work sits at the intersection of some of the most consequential technologies of our time — IoT, Data Analytics, Deep Learning, AI/ML, and Smart Energy. Her research isn't abstract; it reaches hospitals, homes, and industries, exploring how intelligent systems can make everyday life safer, more efficient, and more sustainable.

The recognition for her work has followed. Prof. Silva has been named on the Stanford-Elsevier World's Top 2% Researchers list for 2025, based on her outstanding research publication metrics for 2024. Placing her among a rare global cohort, this achievement also comes as a reminder that world-class research can come from anywhere. Notably, this is not the first time she has earned this distinction, having also appeared on the single-year list in 2023 and 2024.

At home in Sri Lanka, she was awarded ICT Researcher of the Year 2024 by the Computer Society of Sri Lanka, at the National ICT Awards.

But perhaps what's most striking about her story isn't the accolades. It's the arc- a researcher who was shaped by SLIIT, ventured into the world, and came back to help shape the next generation. In a country working hard to build its own research identity on the global stage, this is the kind of journey that inspires hope.
